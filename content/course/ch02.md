# 2. The Vision–Alignment–Strategy Model

<p class='subtitle'>A 3-part framework to diagnose what's holding someone back and map the way forward</p>

<p>The practitioner created the Vision–Alignment–Strategy model to shortcut the coaching process: it helps you diagnose problems and know what to change. It can be applied to your own life and, even more powerfully, to clients. It moves from the <b>Surface-level (presenting) problem</b> to what lies deeper.</p>

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Pillar</th><th>What's missing when it's weak</th><th>Diagnostic question</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>1. Compelling Vision</b></td><td>No clarity on what they want; living in the "I don't know" landscape</td><td>Do you know exactly where you're going?</td></tr><tr><td><b>2. Internal Alignment</b></td><td>One step forward, two or three back; self-sabotage</td><td>Are your beliefs, emotions, behaviours and focus aligned with the goal?</td></tr><tr><td><b>3. Proven Strategy</b></td><td>Making it up as they go; no mentor or plan</td><td>Do you have a guide, a proven plan, and is it working?</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Awareness exercise

<blockquote class='quote'>"Awareness precedes change."</blockquote>

<p>Score each life area, then answer these questions. This alone can make a great <b>First coaching session</b> with a client:</p>

<ol><li>What is your <b>Number one area of growth</b> right now? Describe your current level of performance and results.</li><li>What seems to be holding you back?</li><li>Are you making it harder than it needs to be? Is it that you're <b>Not showing up</b> (self-sabotage; should be 10/10) or that <b>Circumstances are truly beyond you</b> right now? Same result, very different cause.</li><li>What are your <b>Limiting habits</b>?</li><li>What <b>Negative emotional patterns</b> are you engaging in?</li><li>What <b>Beliefs</b> are not serving you?</li><li>What holds your <b>Focus</b>: what you can do or can't do, positive or negative?</li><li>If you change nothing, <b>Where will you be in three years</b>?</li></ol>

## Pillar 1: Compelling Vision

<p><b>Alice in Wonderland</b>: Alice reaches a fork and asks the Cheshire Cat which way to go. The Cat asks where she wants to get to. She says she doesn't much care, so the Cat replies that then it doesn't matter which way she goes. <b>If you don't know where you're going, you'll probably get lost.</b></p>

### 3 reasons people lack a compelling vision

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>#</th><th>Reason</th><th>Example</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1</td><td>They've <b>Never taken the time</b> to consider it</td><td>People plan holidays and weddings more carefully than their lives and marriages.</td></tr><tr><td>2</td><td>They're <b>Afraid to acknowledge</b> what they want because it highlights where they are now</td><td>Wanting to reach 70 kg means admitting you're at 80 kg, and feeling accountable to that.</td></tr><tr><td>3</td><td>They know the goal but feel <b>Powerless</b> to achieve it</td><td>"I don't know how to lose the 10 kg, so I may as well give up." They don't appreciate the power of mentorship.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Pillar 2: Internal Alignment

<p>At practitioner level, internal alignment focuses on four things: <b>Beliefs, emotions, behaviours and focus</b>. The master practitioner level adds values, roles, rules, metaprograms and identity.</p>

### The iceberg analogy

<p>Most people talk about the <b>Presenting problem</b> (above the waterline). A headache is not the problem; it could come from dehydration, caffeine, a tight scrunchie or something serious. Most people just "take an aspirin". In life, the root cause below the surface is usually an <b>Emotion, belief, behaviour or pattern of self-sabotage</b>.</p>

### The self-sabotage formula (very important)

<div class='box key'><div class='box-title'>Key Takeaway</div><p><b>"I want to achieve [GOAL], but I keep self-sabotaging by [PRESENTING PROBLEM / BEHAVIOUR] because deep down I feel or believe [ROOT CAUSE]."</b></p>

<p>Example: "I want to grow my business, but I keep self-sabotaging by <i>procrastinating</i> (not picking up the phone), because deep down I <i>feel inadequate / don't believe I'm worthy</i>."</p>

<p>Tip: frame the behaviour as something you <i>are doing</i> (procrastinating), not something you're not doing. If you can't fill in the goal, the issue is Compelling Vision.</p>

</div>

## Pillar 3: Proven Strategy

<p><b>The cheesecake metaphor</b>: if you tasted an amazing cheesecake at a restaurant, you wouldn't go home, grab random ingredients (eggs, tuna, tomatoes, spinach, oats), bake at a random temperature and hope. Yet we do exactly this with health, money and relationships, copying people who haven't achieved what we want.</p>

<p>To recreate the result you need three things:</p>

<ol><li><b>The chef</b>: a mentor you respect who has done it (insider knowledge)</li><li><b>The recipe and method</b>: the step-by-step process</li><li><b>The ingredients list</b>: the right resources</li></ol>

<p>If you're missing any of the three, you're in trouble. Extra example: he worked 6 months, 7 days a week, unpaid, with a mentor overseas to learn NLP. His twist on the Picasso line: good artists copy, great artists steal, and "stupid people reinvent the wheel". NLP hasn't been reinvented since 1975, just improved.</p>

<div class='box extra'><div class='box-title'>Going deeper</div><p>This is NLP's core idea of <b>Modelling</b>: study someone who already gets the result, identify their strategy (beliefs, internal process, behaviours), and replicate it. Bandler and Grinder built NLP by modelling therapists who got outstanding results (see Chapter 3).</p>

<p>The "good artists copy, great artists steal" line is commonly attributed to Picasso but has no verified source; T.S. Eliot wrote a similar line in 1920.</p>
