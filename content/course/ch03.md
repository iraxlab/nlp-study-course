# 3. History of NLP

<p class='subtitle'>Where NLP came from and who it was modelled on</p>

## What does NLP stand for?

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Word</th><th>Meaning</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>Neuro</b></td><td>The brain and nervous system (anything with neurons)</td></tr><tr><td><b>Linguistic</b></td><td>Language: the language of the brain</td></tr><tr><td><b>Programming</b></td><td>The programs (patterns) that run our thinking and behaviour, like an operating system</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## The founders

<p>Created by <b>Richard Bandler</b> and <b>John Grinder</b> at the University of California, Santa Cruz, in the 1970s. They wanted to know why some people (and some computer programs) run smoothly while others "glitch", and whether learning the brain's language would allow us to change it. NLP is best described as <b>The study of excellence</b>.</p>

<p>Analogy: your phone's hardware stays the same, but the operating system is updated regularly to fix glitches. NLP aims to "update the software" of the brain.</p>

## The three original models

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Person</th><th>Field</th><th>Contribution to NLP</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>Virginia Satir</b></td><td>Family therapy</td><td>Helping couples and families understand what each other really means. Satir categories (communication stances). Her language patterns fed into the Meta Model.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Dr Milton Erickson</b></td><td>Hypnotherapy</td><td>World-famous hypnotherapist. Basis of the <b>Milton Model</b> (artfully vague, hypnotic language).</td></tr><tr><td><b>Fritz Perls</b></td><td>Gestalt therapy</td><td>Focus on the present moment and unfinished business. NLP training links it to later Time Line Therapy (Tad James).</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<p>Today NLP is a collection of tools from many therapies. NLP training calls itself the "next generation" of NLP, combining classic tools with coaching and newer research.</p>

</div>

<div class='box extra'><div class='box-title'>Going deeper</div><p><b>Gregory Bateson</b> (anthropologist, systems thinker) was a key mentor who introduced Bandler and Grinder to Erickson.</p>

<p><b>Alfred Korzybski</b> coined "<b>The map is not the territory</b>", a core NLP presupposition that matches Principle 1 (we respond to our internal map, not reality itself).</p>

<p><b>Meta Model</b> = precise questions to recover deleted, distorted and generalised information ("Who specifically?", "Always?"). <b>Milton Model</b> = the reverse: deliberately vague language used in hypnosis.</p>

<p>Balanced view: NLP is widely used in coaching and sales, but academic psychology regards many of its specific claims as lacking strong scientific evidence. Knowing this helps you speak credibly with sceptical clients.</p>

</div>
