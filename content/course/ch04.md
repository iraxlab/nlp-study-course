# 4. The Three Brains

<p class='subtitle'>Head, heart and gut: the mind-body connection</p>

<p>Original NLP focused only on the head. But "neuro" means anything with neurons, and neurons are also found in the <b>Heart</b> and the <b>Gut</b>. So, in effect, we have three brains.</p>

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th></th><th>Head brain</th><th>Heart brain</th><th>Gut brain</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Type</td><td>Logical brain</td><td>Emotional brain</td><td>Instinctive brain</td></tr><tr><td>Neurons </td><td>50–100 billion</td><td>30,000–120,000 (fewer but tightly bound)</td><td>200–500 million</td></tr><tr><td>Strengths</td><td>Cognition, thinking, making meaning</td><td>Emotions, values, relational connection</td><td>Core identity, self-preservation, mobilisation (action)</td></tr><tr><td>At its best</td><td><b>Creativity</b></td><td><b>Compassion</b></td><td><b>Courage</b></td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<p><b>Gut and mental health</b>: over 80% of the body's serotonin is in the gut. Gut and digestive issues are linked with higher rates of anxiety and depression, so these may sometimes be gut-based rather than purely brain-based problems.</p>

<blockquote class='quote'>"Stir the heart, challenge the mind, call the body to action."</blockquote>

<p>NLP training also mentions the vast number of potential neural connections in the body and links this to quantum ideas: everything is connected as one being.</p>
