# 5. The 3 Power Questions & Holy Trinity of Results

<p class='subtitle'>The questions your mind asks every second, and the three things that control your state</p>

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>#</th><th>Question</th><th>What it's about</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1</td><td><b>What's happening?</b></td><td>Focus. What should I pay attention to? A <b>Choice-based</b> question, because you only see a tiny fraction of reality.</td></tr><tr><td>2</td><td><b>What does it mean to me?</b></td><td>Meaning. Nothing has meaning except the meaning we give it, so choose it wisely (this shapes the emotional response).</td></tr><tr><td>3</td><td><b>What am I going to do about it?</b></td><td>Action. The decision and behaviour that follow.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<ul><li><b>What's happening?</b> At 7–9 years old: Mum and Dad are separating.</li><li><b>What does it mean?</b> Dad won't be around, so the family needs a father figure; Mum and sister are "in danger".</li><li><b>What will I do?</b> "I'll become the man of the house." He went from 7 to "27 or 47" overnight. This was a subconscious decision.</li></ul>

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Order</th><th>Consequence</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1st order</td><td>Became more mature, stronger, serious and self-reliant; modelled his father.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<div class='box key'><div class='box-title'>Key Takeaway</div><p>Every answer to the three questions has <b>1st, 2nd, 3rd (and further) order consequences</b>. Reflect on your <b>Significant Emotional Events (SEEs)</b>: how did you answer the three questions at each high and low in your life?</p>

</div>

<div class='box extra'><div class='box-title'>Going deeper</div><p><b>Second-order thinking</b> is also a well-known decision-making idea (Howard Marks, Shane Parrish): ask "And then what?" repeatedly.</p>

<p>"Significant Emotional Event" comes from sociologist <b>Morris Massey</b>, who described how values are formed during the imprint (0–7), modelling (8–13) and socialisation (14–21) periods.</p>

<p>Question 2 matches the <b>ABC model</b> from cognitive behavioural therapy (Albert Ellis): Activating event → Belief (meaning) → Consequence.</p>

</div>

## Holy Trinity of Results: Focus, Language, Movement

<p><i>The next chapter in NLP training (Section 1, chapter 10). It builds directly on the power questions.</i></p>

<p>Three factors determine the results in your life, because they all determine your <b>Emotional state</b>:</p>

<blockquote class='quote'>State → actions → results</blockquote>

<p>In a positive state you take positive actions and get positive results. In a negative state (frustrated, tired, anxious, low) you take negative actions and get negative results. So how do you control your state? Through <b>Focus, language and movement</b>, an idea from <b>Tony Robbins</b>, whom the practitioner has met and worked with.</p>

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Element</th><th>What it means</th><th>Key points</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>1. Focus</b></td><td>What you pay attention to and the meaning you give it</td><td>Whatever you focus on expands. It answers power questions 1 and 2 (<i>What's happening? What does it mean to me?</i>). Good meaning, good feeling; bad meaning, bad feeling.</td></tr><tr><td><b>2. Language</b></td><td>The words you use to describe your experience</td><td>Language can <b>Diminish</b> or <b>Expand</b> your world. Most people shrink their positives ("not bad", "just okay", "surviving") and blow up their negatives ("a disaster", "she stabbed me in the back", "a nightmare", "a bloodbath on the markets").</td></tr><tr><td><b>3. Movement</b></td><td>Your posture and physiology</td><td>A slumped, head-down posture brings you down; an upright, open, chin-up stance makes you feel strong and confident. The practitioner stands while recording so he doesn't lose energy.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

### Language examples

<ul><li><b>Media language</b>: a reporter asked whether children could pass the virus to elderly relatives, but framed it as children being a "human Petri dish". Sensational words ramp up fear; no wonder people who watch a lot of news feel negative.</li><li><b>"How are you?"</b> Answer "Not bad" and you shrink your emotional range. Answer "Sensational! Best day ever!" and you expand it, and it's infectious.</li><li>People use language either to make their problems feel bigger (more significant) or to constantly diminish things. Notice the words you use <b>Habitually</b>.</li></ul>

### Movement: the "Harvard study"

<p>The practitioner describes a Harvard study: an <b>"alpha" pose</b> (strong, expanded stance, chin up, body open) raised <b>Testosterone</b> (strength, confidence) and lowered <b>Cortisol</b> (stress); a <b>Defeated posture</b> did the opposite, because "the body doesn't know it's not true".</p>

<div class='box key'><div class='box-title'>Key Takeaway</div><p>Negative focus + bad posture + negative language = feeling average and achieving little.</p>

<p>Focus on what you <b>Can</b> do with what you have + positive language + taking responsibility + an energised body = the right state to "kick some goals". <b>Master focus, language and movement.</b></p>

</div>

<p>Tony Robbins calls this <b>"the Triad"</b>: <b>Physiology, focus and language</b>.</p>

</div>

<div class='box extra'><div class='box-title'>Going deeper</div><p>Robbins' related idea is <b>Transformational vocabulary</b>: deliberately softening words for negative states ("a bit peeved" instead of "furious") and intensifying words for positive ones ("unstoppable" instead of "okay").</p>

<p>This mirrors Chapter 13 (Frames): the same event framed as a "bloodbath" or as "everything's on sale".</p>

<p>Research on <b>Upright posture</b> (e.g. Nair et al., 2015) found people sitting upright under stress reported more self-esteem and less fear than slumped sitters, a modest but real effect.</p>

</div>
