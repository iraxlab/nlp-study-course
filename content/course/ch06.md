# 6. Criteria for Creating Outcomes

<p class='subtitle'>Nine checks to make sure a client's goal is well-formed</p>

<p>Use these nine criteria whenever you set goals with a client, so the outcome is aligned with a higher vision or purpose.</p>

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>#</th><th>Criterion</th><th>Explanation</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1</td><td><b>Stated in the positive</b></td><td>Most people say what they <i>don't</i> want. Turn it into what they <i>do</i> want ("I want to feel calm", not "I don't want to be stressed").</td></tr><tr><td>2</td><td><b>Ecological</b></td><td>Good for the client, good for you (the coach), good for the planet / others.</td></tr><tr><td>3</td><td><b>Self-led</b></td><td>The client must choose it and be able to start, maintain and manage it themselves. Telling them what to do doesn't work. Consider their unique environment.</td></tr><tr><td>4</td><td><b>Associate to the end step</b></td><td>Have them step into the result as if it's happening now: what do they see, hear, feel?</td></tr><tr><td>5</td><td><b>Dissociate from the end step</b></td><td>Step back out to create drive and determination: the "emotional elastic band" between now and the goal.</td></tr><tr><td>6</td><td><b>Confirm ecology</b></td><td>Check again: good for me, good for others, good for the planet.</td></tr><tr><td>7</td><td><b>Many ways to get there</b></td><td>1 + 1 = 2, but so does 4 − 2. Avoid tunnel vision.</td></tr><tr><td>8</td><td><b>Realistic</b></td><td>The next step must be something they can realistically do.</td></tr><tr><td>9</td><td><b>Increases choice</b></td><td>More choice = an expansive state. Less choice = feeling cut off and frustrated.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<p><b>Memory hook:</b> Positive, Ecological, Self-led, Associate, Dissociate, Ecology check, Many ways, Realistic, More choice.</p>

<div class='box extra'><div class='box-title'>Going deeper</div><p>In classic NLP this is called <b>Well-Formed Outcomes</b>. A typical version: (1) stated in the positive, (2) sensory-based evidence ("How will you know you've got it?"), (3) self-initiated and maintained, (4) context defined (when, where, with whom), (5) resources identified, (6) preserves the positive by-products of the current situation, (7) ecological, (8) first step identified.</p>

<p>Useful <b>Ecology questions</b>: What will you gain if you get it? What will you lose? What happens if you don't get it? What won't happen if you do?</p>

<p>Associated = seeing through your own eyes (strong emotion). Dissociated = seeing yourself from the outside (less emotion, more perspective).</p>
