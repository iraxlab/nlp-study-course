# 7. The 7 Human Needs

<p class='subtitle'>What drives every human behaviour (adapted from Tony Robbins' 6 Human Needs)</p>

<p>Tony Robbins teaches <b>Six</b> human needs; some trainings add a seventh, <b>Love</b>, as the highest. Everyone meets all needs somehow, but we usually lean towards one side of each pair.</p>

## The four needs of the personality (two opposing pairs)

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Need</th><th>Meaning</th><th>Often looks like</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>Certainty</b></td><td>Knowing what comes next; security</td><td>Same food, same routines, secure jobs (government, large corporations)</td></tr><tr><td><b>Variety</b> (uncertainty)</td><td>"The spice of life": adventure, novelty</td><td>Travel, exotic restaurants, trying new things</td></tr><tr><td><b>Connection</b></td><td>Belonging to the group / tribe</td><td>Being the one who brings people together</td></tr><tr><td><b>Significance</b></td><td>Standing out, feeling special and important</td><td>Leading or running the group, being praised</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<div class='box key'><div class='box-title'>Key Takeaway</div><p><b>The addiction rule:</b> if something meets <b>Three or more needs at a high level (8–10/10)</b>, you will become addicted to it, positively (work you love, a great relationship) or negatively (drugs, gambling, toxic relationships).</p>

<p>Extra example: his work gives him 10/10 certainty (expertise), variety (every client is different, travel), connection (live events) and significance (on stage), which is why he's obsessed with it.</p>

</div>

## The needs of the spirit

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Need</th><th>Meaning</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>Growth</b></td><td>Once your cup is full, you want a bigger cup: personal and professional development.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Contribution</b></td><td>Giving beyond yourself and making a difference in others' lives.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Love</b> (added in some trainings)</td><td>The ultimate highest value. When something meets all the needs, you love it (and can be "in love" with a person or your work). When needs stop being met, love fades.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<p>Most people have one dominant need and a strong second. The practitioner's top two: <b>Growth and contribution</b>.</p>

<div class='box extra'><div class='box-title'>Going deeper</div><p><b>Coaching tip:</b> ask which needs a client's problem behaviour is meeting. A new behaviour must meet those needs at least as well, or the old one will return.</p>

<p>Robbins developed this with <b>Cloé Madanes</b> (Human Needs Psychology / Strategic Intervention). In Robbins' model, the first four are "needs of the personality" and growth and contribution are "needs of the spirit".</p>

<p>Comparison with <b>Maslow's hierarchy</b> (1943): physiological → safety → love/belonging → esteem → self-actualisation. Certainty ≈ safety, connection ≈ belonging, significance ≈ esteem, growth ≈ self-actualisation, contribution ≈ self-transcendence (Maslow's later addition).</p>

</div>
