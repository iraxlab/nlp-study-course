# 10. Rapport

<p class='subtitle'>Communication Mastery: building a deep connection with anyone, fast</p>

<p><b>Rapport</b> is a deep, harmonious connection with another person: the feeling of having "known them forever". Sometimes it happens automatically; sometimes, however long you talk, you can't get on the same page. The skill of rapport turns the second experience into the first. Its two pillars are <b>Mirroring</b> and <b>Matching</b>.</p>

## Mirroring vs. matching

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th></th><th>Mirroring</th><th>Matching</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>What it is</td><td>Like looking in a mirror: they raise their <b>Right</b> hand, you raise your <b>Left</b></td><td>Doing the same thing on the same side: they raise their <b>Right</b> hand, you raise your <b>Right</b></td></tr><tr><td>Awareness level </td><td>More <b>Conscious</b> (we're used to seeing our mirror image)</td><td>More <b>Subconscious</b>, and more powerful</td></tr><tr><td>Use</td><td>Use both</td><td>Use both</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## The three channels to mirror and match

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Channel</th><th>Share of communication </th><th>What to match</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>Physiology</b> (body language)</td><td><b>55%</b></td><td>Posture, gestures, facial expressions, blinking, breathing</td></tr><tr><td><b>Tonality</b> (how words are said)</td><td><b>38%</b></td><td>Tone, tempo (speed), timbre, volume. Fast talker → speed up; slow, soft speaker → slow down and soften.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Words</b> (what is said)</td><td><b>7%</b></td><td>Predicates (VAK + auditory digital), keywords, chunk size, common experiences</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

### Matching words

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Word element</th><th>Explanation and examples</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>Predicates</b></td><td>Sensory words that reveal how someone thinks. <b>Visual</b>: "Can you <i>see</i> where I'm going?" · <b>Auditory</b>: "How does that <i>sound</i>?" · <b>Kinaesthetic</b>: "Are you <i>feeling</i> me?" · <b>Auditory digital</b> (logic, self-talk): "Does that <i>make sense</i>?"</td></tr><tr><td><b>Keywords</b></td><td>Words a person uses often: nicknames ("bae"), "bud" or "buddy". Use their words back.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Common experiences</b></td><td>"You're from England? So am I." Useful, but most people think it's the <i>only</i> way to build rapport. It isn't.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## The 4 indicators of rapport

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>#</th><th>Indicator</th><th>What it looks like</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1</td><td><b>Internal feeling</b></td><td>A sense that you just "get" each other</td></tr><tr><td>2</td><td><b>Colour change</b></td><td>A visible shift such as flushing in the face (links to Sensory Acuity)</td></tr><tr><td>3</td><td><b>They say something</b></td><td>"I feel like I've known you forever" / "Have we met before?" (optional, but common)</td></tr><tr><td>4</td><td><b>Leading</b></td><td>The ultimate test: <b>When you change, they change</b>. You lean forward, they lean forward; you cross or uncross your arms or legs, they follow.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<p>All of this already happens unconsciously. NLP takes it and applies <b>Conscious volition</b> (deliberate choice) so you can create rapport faster.</p>

## How to build rapport: guidelines

<ul><li>Start by noticing their body language, then pick <b>One or two aspects</b> to match. If matching the legs is awkward (e.g. wearing a skirt), match the <b>Upper body</b> only: leaning back or forward, arms crossed, gestures.</li><li><b>Be authentic, not a parrot.</b> In France you'd try to speak French, not speak English in a fake French accent. Match respectfully so they feel comfortable; don't mimic or mock.</li><li>Match pace, softness and pronunciation of speech, and pick up and use their words.</li><li>Don't lose yourself. Experiment within a range that still feels comfortable to you.</li><li>Think of rapport as a <b>Dance</b>: first you follow (pacing), then you lead.</li></ul>

<blockquote class='quote'>Pace, pace, pace... then lead.</blockquote>

<div class='box key'><div class='box-title'>Key Takeaway</div><p>Not "treat people the way <i>you</i> want to be treated", but <b>Communicate with people the way <i>they</i> want to be communicated with</b>. As a coach you must <b>Meet clients where they are, then lead them towards transformation</b>.</p>

</div>

<p>The practitioner's version of the Golden Rule alternative is usually called the <b>Platinum Rule</b> (Tony Alessandra): treat others the way <i>they</i> want to be treated.</p>

</div>

<div class='box extra'><div class='box-title'>Going deeper</div><p><b>The chameleon effect</b> (Chartrand &amp; Bargh, 1999): people unconsciously mimic each other, and people who were mimicked liked their partner more and felt the interaction went more smoothly. In a later study (van Baaren et al., 2003), waitresses who repeated customers' orders back word for word received noticeably larger tips.</p>

<p><b>Cross-over matching</b> (indirect matching): matching a behaviour with a different part of the body, e.g. tapping a finger in time with someone's breathing. It's subtler than direct mirroring.</p>

<p><b>Mismatching</b> is useful too: breaking rapport deliberately (turning your body away, changing pace) is a polite way to end a conversation.</p>

<p><b>Representational systems</b> in full are <b>VAKOG</b>: Visual, Auditory, Kinaesthetic, Olfactory (smell), Gustatory (taste), plus <b>Ad</b> (auditory digital: self-talk and logic). People often favour one system, which shows in their predicates.</p>

<p><b>Chunking</b> (coming up in NLP training): <b>Chunk up</b> to the big picture and purpose ("What is this for?"); <b>Chunk down</b> to specifics ("What specifically?"); <b>Chunk sideways</b> to examples ("What's another example?").</p>

<p>Rapport is often linked to <b>Mirror neurons</b> (Rizzolatti, 1990s), though how much they explain human empathy is still debated.</p>

</div>
