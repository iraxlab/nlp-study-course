# 14. Questions to Customise Results

<p class='subtitle'>Communication Mastery: coaching questions to get a client crystal clear on their goal</p>

<p>A set of questions to use <b>At the start of a coaching process</b>. They give the client clarity on where they are, where they want to be, and the pros and cons of every action and inaction. Keep them in the back of your mind rather than reading them as a script.</p>

## Part 1: Clarifying the outcome

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>#</th><th>Question</th><th>What it does</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1</td><td><b>What specifically do you want?</b></td><td>Basic, but many people don't know. (Chunk down.)</td></tr><tr><td>2</td><td><b>Where are you now?</b></td><td>Establishes the present state.</td></tr><tr><td>3</td><td><b>What will you see, hear, feel (taste, smell) when you have it?</b></td><td>Makes the goal sensory and vivid (VAKOG). Builds awareness of the gap between now and the goal.</td></tr><tr><td>4</td><td><b>How will you know when you have it?</b></td><td>The <b>Evidence procedure</b>: what the client will accept as proof they've succeeded.</td></tr><tr><td>5</td><td><b>What will this outcome get for you or allow you to do?</b></td><td>Chunks up to purpose and second-order consequences.</td></tr><tr><td>6</td><td><b>Is it only for you?</b></td><td>Who else is involved? Can you rely on them, and use them as motivation?</td></tr><tr><td>7</td><td><b>Where, when, how and with whom do you want it?</b></td><td>Defines context; enrols other people and factors.</td></tr><tr><td>8</td><td><b>What do you have now, and what do you need to get your outcome?</b></td><td>Identifies resources and gaps.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Part 2: Resources, purpose and ecology

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>#</th><th>Question</th><th>What it does</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>9</td><td><b>Have you ever had or done this before?</b></td><td>Finds past success to draw on.</td></tr><tr><td>10</td><td><b>Do you know anyone who has?</b></td><td>Finds a model or mentor (proven strategy).</td></tr><tr><td>11</td><td><b>Can you act as if you have it?</b></td><td>The "as if" frame: rehearses the outcome now.</td></tr><tr><td>12</td><td><b>For what purpose do you want this?</b></td><td>Purpose frame: the real desire behind the goal.</td></tr><tr><td>13</td><td><b>What will you gain or lose if you have it?</b></td><td>Ecology: hidden costs of success.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

### The 4 Cartesian (ecology) questions

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th></th><th>If you <b>GET</b> it</th><th>If you <b>DON'T GET</b> it</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>Will happen</b></td><td>What <b>Will</b> happen if you get it?</td><td>What <b>Will</b> happen if you don't get it?</td></tr><tr><td><b>Won't happen</b></td><td>What <b>Won't</b> happen if you get it?</td><td>What <b>Won't</b> happen if you don't get it?</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<p>The practitioner calls these <b>Cartesian logic questions</b>: they look at the goal from all four angles, so the client sees the full picture of action and inaction. The last one ("What won't happen if you don't get it?") is deliberately confusing, and often surfaces hidden benefits of staying stuck.</p>

<div class='box key'><div class='box-title'>Key Takeaway</div><p>Use these questions at the <b>Start of coaching</b> to make the goal specific, sensory, measurable, ecological and owned by the client.</p>

</div>

<div class='box extra'><div class='box-title'>Going deeper</div><p>These are the classic NLP <b>Well-formed outcome</b> questions, which map onto the 9 Criteria for Creating Outcomes (Chapter 6): positive, specific, sensory evidence, self-led, context, resources, ecology.</p>

<p>The four quadrants are named after the four ways of combining a statement in logic, sometimes called <b>Cartesian coordinates</b> in NLP. The confusing quadrant works by overloading the conscious mind so the answer comes from deeper down.</p>

<p>Compare with <b>SMART goals</b> (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound, from George Doran, 1981). The NLP questions add the sensory and ecology checks that SMART leaves out.</p>

<p>Question 12's "hidden benefit" is often called <b>Secondary gain</b>: what the current problem is giving the client (attention, safety, an excuse). If the new outcome doesn't meet that need, the old behaviour tends to come back (see the 7 Human Needs).</p>
