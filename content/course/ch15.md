# 15. Representational Systems & Predicates

<p class='subtitle'>Communication Mastery: how your clients build their picture of reality</p>

<p>Everyone processes information through <b>Representational systems</b>: in other words, the senses. Visual, auditory, kinaesthetic, gustatory and olfactory (seeing, hearing, touching, tasting, smelling), <b>Plus self-talk</b> (internal dialogue, called <b>Auditory digital</b>).</p>

<p>We perceive the outside world, then translate it into an internal blueprint: an <b>Internal Representation (IR)</b>. It's a representation of the world, our unique version of reality, not the world itself.</p>

## The four primary systems

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>System</th><th>Builds IRs through</th><th>Short code</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>Visual</b></td><td>Pictures</td><td>V</td></tr><tr><td><b>Auditory</b></td><td>Sounds</td><td>A</td></tr><tr><td><b>Kinaesthetic</b></td><td>Feelings</td><td>K</td></tr><tr><td><b>Auditory digital</b></td><td>Self-talk, logic, "making sense"</td><td>Ad</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<p>We use <b>All four</b>, but most people lean on some more than others, a <b>Dominant hierarchy</b>. The one used most is the <b>Preferred representational system</b>. So people are often described as "visual", "auditory", "kinaesthetic" or "auditory digital" people.</p>

## How to spot it: predicates

<p>People reveal their system through <b>Predicates</b> (sensory words) and <b>Predicate phrases</b>. When someone uses this language, it's a clear indicator of how they're processing the world <b>In that moment</b>.</p>

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th></th><th>Visual (V)</th><th>Auditory (A)</th><th>Kinaesthetic (K)</th><th>Auditory Digital (Ad)</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Words</td><td>Appear, clear, crystal, dawn, envision, focused, foggy, hazy, illuminate, imagine, look, picture, reveal, see</td><td>All ears, deaf, dissonance, harmonise, hear, listen, make music, question, resonate, rings a bell, silence, sound(s), tune in/out, unhearing</td><td>Catch on, concrete, feel, feel out, grasp, get a handle, get hold of, hard, harsh, make contact, sense, sensitive, solid, tap into</td><td>Change, chart progress, conceive, consider, criteria, decide, distinct, experience, know, learn, makes sense, motivate, perceive, plan</td></tr><tr><td>Phrases</td><td>An eyeful, appears to me, bird's-eye view, catch a glimpse of, caught my eye, clear-cut, easy on the eyes, eye candy, eyes on the prize, flashed on, from another perspective, get the big picture, hazy notion, horse of a different colour, in light of, in person, in view of, just looking, laid eyes on</td><td>Blabbermouth, clear as a bell, call on, earful, falls on deaf ears, grant an audience, hear me out, hearsay, hear what I'm saying, heard it all, heard it through the grapevine, hidden message, hold your tongue, I hear you, idle chatter, keynote speaker, listen to this</td><td>All washed up, boils down to, chip off the old block, come to grips with, control yourself, cool calm and collected, feel where I'm coming from, firm foundations, get a handle on, get a load of this, get a sense of things, get over it, get the drift, gut feeling, hang in there, have a feeling, heated argument</td><td>According to, afterthought, clearly expressed, compared to, describe in detail, give an account of, figure of speech, fits the criteria, good point, inner voice, inquire into, know-how, listen to myself, mental note, on second thought, pay attention to, pros and cons, relatively speaking</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Why it matters

<ul><li>It's the <b>Foundational building block</b> of how a person interprets the world around them.</li><li>Mismatches cause misunderstandings: someone may <b>Hear</b> words and then <b>Make feelings</b> about them that don't match what was meant. Or they <b>See</b> a situation and attach an emotion that wasn't intended.</li><li>We assume our interpretation of the world is accurate. In reality it <b>Rarely is</b>: many variables are at work, plus the systems we lean on more than others (links to delete / distort / generalise, Chapter 1).</li><li>If you understand the client's model of the world, you can <b>Pace them, lead them and build rapport</b> with them (Chapter 10).</li></ul>

<div class='box key'><div class='box-title'>Key Takeaway</div><p>Listen for a client's go-to predicates, then <b>Answer in their system</b>: "I <i>see</i> what you mean" (V) · "That <i>sounds</i> right" (A) · "I <i>feel</i> that too" (K) · "That <i>makes sense</i>" (Ad). Be aware of <b>Your own</b> preferred system as well as theirs.</p>

</div>

## What each system does

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>System</th><th>What it does</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>Visual (V)</b>: creates and sees pictures</td><td>Holds the largest amount of data at once, instantly. "A picture is worth a thousand words."</td></tr><tr><td><b>Auditory (A)</b>: hears sounds</td><td>More sequential; takes longer to process. Adds emphasis or contrast to images, like a film's soundtrack.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Kinaesthetic (K)</b>: feels</td><td>Feelings inside the body (proprioceptive) and on the skin (tactile): pure sensation without evaluation. Can last after the pictures and sounds fade. <b>Meta-kinaesthetic</b> is the overall "sense" of something: the general positive or negative value we give an event.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Auditory digital (Ad)</b>: self-talk</td><td>A secondary interpretation: what we say to ourselves about events and what they mean. Summarises and sorts experience into words.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<p>Some experiences suit one system: music is mostly auditory, yet some people see vivid images while listening and others feel the mood. A massage is mostly kinaesthetic, yet some people run a commentary in their head the whole time.</p>

## Behaviour clues for each preferred system

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Preferred system</th><th>Typical clues</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>Visual</b></td><td>Operates from the top half of the body: breathes high in the chest and speaks faster; sits or stands upright, eyes up, often at the front of the chair. Appearance matters, so often neat and organised. Learns by linking images to ideas; struggles with spoken-only instructions; not easily distracted by noise; has to see something to get interested.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Auditory</b></td><td>Breathes from the middle of the chest; eyes move side to side; often talks to themselves; easily distracted by noise. Learns by hearing information in sequence; likes to be told how they're doing; feels loved through tone of voice and words; enjoys music and phone calls.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Kinaesthetic</b></td><td>Breathes low, so the stomach visibly moves; moves and speaks slowly; responds to touch and physical rewards; learns by doing. Something has to "feel right" before they're interested.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Auditory digital</b></td><td>Listens to an inner dialogue; makes decisions with a list of criteria; can use any of the other systems, but gets interested when something "makes sense".</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<div class='box extra'><div class='box-title'>Going deeper</div><p>Common descriptions (useful, but stereotypes): <b>V</b> speak fast, breathe high, care how things look; <b>A</b> sensitive to tone and noise, like to talk things through; <b>K</b> speak slowly, breathe low, need to feel it before deciding; <b>Ad</b> need things to make logical sense, like data and steps.</p>

<p>Gustatory (taste) and olfactory (smell) are full systems too (<b>VAKOG</b>), but they're used less often in language: "a bitter pill", "that deal smells fishy".</p>

<p><b>Eye accessing cues</b> are often taught alongside this (eyes up = visual, sideways = auditory, down-right = feelings, down-left = self-talk, for most right-handed people).</p>

<div class='box fact'><div class='box-title'>Fact check</div><ul><li>Research has <b>not</b> supported the claim that people have one fixed preferred system you can reliably detect, or that eye movements reveal thinking style or lying (e.g. Wiseman et al., 2012). The related "VAK learning styles" idea in education is also considered a myth. This fits: predicates show how someone is processing <i>in that moment</i>. Matching someone's words is still a sound rapport habit; just treat preferences as tendencies, not types.</li></ul></div>
