# 18. The Clarity Model (Meta Model)

<p class='subtitle'>Communication Mastery: the antidote to hypnotic language</p>

<p>The practitioner calls the Clarity Model <b>"the antidote to hypnotic language"</b>. In NLP it's known as the <b>Meta Model</b>: a way of understanding how people process the world and <b>Challenging the built-in deletions, distortions and generalisations</b> in what they say.</p>

## Why it works

<ul><li>Remember Chapter 1: <b>2 million bits</b> of information hit us every second. The brain can't process it all, so it filters by <b>Deleting, distorting and generalising</b>.</li><li>Those filters are then <b>Built into the way we talk</b>. <b>The words we use are the mirror reflection of our internal filters.</b></li><li>So you can hear from someone's language whether they've deleted, distorted or generalised. Then you can challenge the filter, letting them see a different slice of that <b>0.0032% of reality</b>.</li></ul>

## The three groups and the master questions

## The 12 patterns and how to challenge them

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>#</th><th>Pattern</th><th>What it is</th><th>Example</th><th>Response (standard version + Extra extras)</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1</td><td><b>Mind reading</b> (D)</td><td>Claiming to know someone's internal state</td><td>"He's upset with me."</td><td>How do you know he's upset with you?</td></tr><tr><td>2</td><td><b>Lost performative</b> (D)</td><td>A value judgement where the person judging is left out</td><td>"Not speaking up is bad."</td><td>Who says it's bad? According to whom? How do you know it's bad?</td></tr><tr><td>3</td><td><b>Cause &amp; effect</b> (D)</td><td>The cause is wrongly put <b>Outside the self</b> (not taking extreme ownership)</td><td>"He makes me mad."</td><td>How does what he's doing cause you to <i>choose</i> to feel mad? How specifically? What is he doing specifically?</td></tr><tr><td>4</td><td><b>Complex equivalence</b> (D)</td><td>Two experiences interpreted as meaning the same</td><td>"He comes home late. He's irresponsible."</td><td>How does coming home late mean he's irresponsible? Has anyone ever been late, yet still responsible?</td></tr><tr><td>5</td><td><b>Presuppositions</b> (D)</td><td>Hidden assumptions in the sentence</td><td>"If my husband knew how lonely I get, he wouldn't do that."</td><td>Three presuppositions: a) <i>I am lonely</i> → How do you choose to feel lonely? b) <i>He acts in some way</i> → How is he reacting? c) <i>He doesn't know</i> → How do you know he doesn't know?</td></tr><tr><td>6</td><td><b>Universal quantifiers</b> (G)</td><td>All, every, never, no one, everyone, always</td><td>"I always get it wrong."</td><td>Find a counter-example: "Always?" · What would happen if you didn't get it wrong?</td></tr><tr><td>7a</td><td><b>Modal operators of necessity</b> (G)</td><td>Should, shouldn't, must, must not, have to, need to, it is necessary</td><td>"You have to listen."</td><td>Find a counter-example: What would happen if you did? What would happen if you didn't? Or simply: "<b>Or?</b>"</td></tr><tr><td>7b</td><td><b>Modal operators of possibility / impossibility</b> (G)</td><td>Can / can't, will / won't, may / may not, possible / impossible</td><td>"I can't move yet."</td><td>What prevents you? What would happen if you did? ("I can't quit my job" / "I can't ask them out" → What would happen if you did?)</td></tr><tr><td>8</td><td><b>Nominalisations</b> (Del)</td><td>Process words frozen in time, made into nouns</td><td>"Our relationship doesn't work."</td><td>What specifically isn't working about the way you're <i>relating</i>? How would you like to relate?</td></tr><tr><td>9</td><td><b>Unspecified verbs</b> (Del)</td><td>The action isn't specified</td><td>"She just gave up."</td><td>How specifically? On what specifically?</td></tr><tr><td>10</td><td><b>Simple deletions</b> (Del)</td><td>Important information left out</td><td>"I'm bored."</td><td>With what or whom, specifically?</td></tr><tr><td>11</td><td><b>Lack of referential index</b> (Del)</td><td>Fails to specify a person or thing</td><td>"They don't understand me." · "They always say..."</td><td>Who specifically doesn't understand you? Who's "they"?</td></tr><tr><td>12</td><td><b>Comparative deletion</b> (Del)</td><td>Good, better, best, worst, more, less, most, least</td><td>"I want a better husband."</td><td>Better than whom? Better at what? Compared to whom or what?</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<p>D = Distortion · G = Generalisation · Del = Deletion</p>

<div class='box key'><div class='box-title'>Key Takeaway</div><p><b>Two steps:</b> (1) first <b>Understand</b> what the client is saying and spot the pattern; (2) then use the response to <b>Challenge the built-in assumption</b>. Start with <b>One or two</b> patterns and use them <b>With volition</b> (on purpose).</p>

<p>Cause &amp; effect challenge puts the power back with the client: <b>"How does X cause you to <i>choose</i> to feel Y?"</b> This links directly to responsibility (response + ability), Extreme Ownership and the 90/10 rule.</p>

## Practice: challenge these

What specifically would you be failing at, and how?"</td></tr><tr><td>"I can't speak in public."</td><td>Modal of impossibility → "What prevents you? What would happen if you did?"</td></tr><tr><td>"Things just aren't working out."</td><td>Lack of referential index + unspecified verb → "What things specifically? How specifically aren't they working out?"</td></tr><tr><td>"I need more confidence."</td><td>Modal of necessity + comparative deletion + nominalisation → "More than what? How would you like to be feeling confident, and when?"</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<div class='box extra'><div class='box-title'>Going deeper</div><p>The Clarity Model is NLP's <b>Meta Model</b>, the first model Bandler and Grinder published (<i>The Structure of Magic</i>, 1975), based on how Satir and Perls questioned clients.</p>

<p><b>Nominalisation test</b>: if you can't put it in a wheelbarrow, it's probably a nominalisation (relationship, depression, success, communication). Turn it back into a verb to make it changeable ("relating", "depressing yourself about...").</p>

<p><b>Use with rapport and softeners</b>. Fired one after another, these questions feel like an interrogation. Soften them: "I'm curious, what specifically...?" or "I wonder how you know..."</p>

<p>Don't challenge everything. Pick the pattern that's holding the client's problem in place; usually universal quantifiers, modal operators and cause &amp; effect have the most leverage.</p>

<p>Similar to <b>Socratic questioning</b> in CBT, where therapists ask "What's the evidence for that?" to test unhelpful thoughts.</p>
