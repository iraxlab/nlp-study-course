# 25. Collapse Anchor

<p class='subtitle'>Emotional Mastery: dissolving a negative state by engulfing it in a positive one</p>

<p>A collapse anchor takes an existing <b>Negative</b> state and <b>Engulfs it in a positive state until the negativity dissolves</b>. The old trigger becomes neurologically linked to a more resourceful state, neutralising its old association. You can do it for yourself or for someone else.</p>

<p>It's especially useful when an <b>External trigger</b> (a place, person or situation) sends someone into an unresourceful state. Examples:</p>

<ul><li>Someone who always gets <b>Angry in traffic</b> collapses it with amusement and laughter. Next time, the "negative charge" is gone; they may even find themselves smiling for no apparent reason.</li><li>A <b>Salesperson</b> who gets nervous every time they visit one particular client.</li><li>A <b>Manager</b> who flies off the handle with one particular employee.</li></ul>

<p><b>It's a precision anchor plus one extra step.</b> Watch the precision anchoring example (Chapter 23) first: part one is precision anchoring, part two is the collapse.</p>

## Choosing the states

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Negative (mild)</th><th>Positive replacement </th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Frustration</td><td>Excitement or calmness</td></tr><tr><td>Nervousness / anxiety</td><td>Excitement</td></tr><tr><td>Anger</td><td>Inner peace</td></tr><tr><td>Laziness</td><td>Motivation</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<ul><li>Find an <b>Opposite</b>: a negative to get rid of and a positive to replace it. It should be <b>Self-led</b> by the client.</li><li><b>Only for mild negatives</b>: frustration, annoyance, irritation, laziness, procrastination, doubt, mild fear. Not for hurt, disgust, resentment, depression or anxiety; those are "too big for this exercise" and are covered by bigger tools elsewhere.</li><li><b>Be in the state yourself.</b> An advanced anchoring tip: mirror the emotional state you want the client in. If you're not excited when you're talking, they won't be either.</li></ul>

## The steps

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>#</th><th>Step </th><th>Notes</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1</td><td><b>Choose the negative state</b> to eliminate</td><td>Ask how it's a problem and what it would mean to be free of it.</td></tr><tr><td>2</td><td><b>Choose the powerful positive state(s)</b> to replace it</td><td>One opposite emotion, or several.</td></tr><tr><td>3</td><td><b>Elicit and anchor the positive</b> on one knuckle, <b>At least 5 times</b></td><td>Either one state 5 times, or 5 different states once each (an <b>Anchor stack</b>, e.g. happy, loved, joy, excitement), or a mix, <b>All on the same knuckle</b>. Use the full 5 → 10 ladder, double it, release.</td></tr><tr><td>4</td><td><b>Break state</b></td><td>Open your eyes, close your eyes; or an odd question ("What's your necklace?").</td></tr><tr><td>5</td><td><b>Elicit and anchor the negative</b> on the next knuckle, <b>Once only</b></td><td><b>No build-up.</b> Remember a specific time, notice it, anchor, deep breath, release. Timing matters less here.</td></tr><tr><td>6</td><td><b>Break state</b></td><td></td></tr><tr><td>7</td><td><b>Fire both together</b> until integration is complete</td><td>Talk them into the negative memory while you also bring up the positive feelings.</td></tr><tr><td>8</td><td><b>Release the negative</b>, keep holding the positive</td><td>"Double down" on the positive.</td></tr><tr><td>9</td><td><b>Release the positive about 5 seconds later</b></td><td>While still holding it, build it all the way up again: 5 → 10, double it, deep breath, release. Repeat the collapse 2–3 times.</td></tr><tr><td>10</td><td><b>Test</b></td><td>"So the next time you [encounter the trigger], what happens?"</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<div class='box key'><div class='box-title'>Key Takeaway</div><p><b>Make the good guy big, keep the bad guy small.</b> "If you're going to kill the monster, kill it when it's little, not when it's Godzilla." Build up the positive; never build up the negative.</p>

<p>Ratio: <b>At least 5 positive : 1 negative</b>. "Do that and the process takes care of 90% of the heavy lifting." (For a mild negative, at least 3:1.)</p>

<p>Fire both → <b>Release the negative</b> → double down on the positive → build it up → release.</p>

</div>

### Why it works (Extra explanation)

<p>Imagine injecting a small dose of <b>Disease</b> and a huge vial of <b>Antidote</b> at the same time: the antidote engulfs the disease. Emotions create neural pathways "like spider webs" that strengthen each time they fire. Firing the negative lights up those connection points; engulfing them in the positive <b>Rewires</b> them, so the old trigger now fires the positive. The two anchors <b>Collapse into one</b>.</p>

### Doing it alone: the hula hoop (advanced)

<p>Visualise a <b>Hula hoop</b> on the floor in front of you. Step in and anchor positive emotions five times; step back and get clear on the negative; then step into the hoop again, anchoring yourself multiple times with the positive. A spatial version of the same process, taught live.</p>

<p><b>You can't really mess this up</b>: anything you do can be undone with the positive anchor later.</p>

### Common questions

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Question</th><th>Answer</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Which knuckles?</td><td>It doesn't matter. Just keep one for positive and one for negative, and keep them consistent.</td></tr><tr><td>One positive emotion or several?</td><td>Either: one emotion 5–6 times, or an anchor stack of 5–6 different energised positive emotions. Aim for at least 5 positive to 1 negative.</td></tr><tr><td>How many times do I collapse?</td><td>A couple of times, then test. Then you can move on to other tools.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<p><b>Releasing the positive:</b> one version says release the positive <b>5 seconds</b> after the negative; another version builds the positive back up (5 → 10, double it) before releasing. They fit together: hold the positive, build it, then let go.</p>

<div class='box extra'><div class='box-title'>Going deeper</div><p>The same idea exists in psychology. In 1924 <b>Mary Cover Jones</b> helped a boy, "Peter", lose his fear of rabbits by bringing a rabbit closer while he ate food he loved: <b>Counterconditioning</b>, pairing a trigger with a strong positive response.</p>

<p>Joseph Wolpe's <b>Reciprocal inhibition</b>: you can't feel two opposite states fully at once (e.g. deeply relaxed and anxious), so a stronger positive state crowds out the negative. That's why the positive must be bigger.</p>

<p>The hula hoop exercise resembles the classic NLP <b>Circle of Excellence</b>, a spatial anchor you step into.</p>

<p>As NLP training says, this tool is for mild emotions. Ongoing anxiety, depression or trauma deserve proper professional support.</p>
