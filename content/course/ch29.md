# 29. Belief Change

<p class='subtitle'>Removing a limiting belief and installing a new one, using the like to dislike process twice</p>

<p>This is called one of his favourite NLP exercises. Removing a limiting belief takes about <b>15 minutes</b>; installing a new one about <b>10</b>. Allow about <b>30 minutes</b>. Much of his time with a client goes on the <b>Pre-frame</b>, because "any tool is only as effective as the client's belief in it".</p>

## Part A: Pre-framing

### Find the belief: the self-sabotage sentence again

<p>First seen in Chapter 2. The root cause at the end is usually an <b>Emotion</b> or a <b>Belief</b>:</p>

<blockquote class='quote'>"I want to achieve [goal], but I keep self-sabotaging by [behaviour] because deep down I feel or believe [root cause]."</blockquote>

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Goal</th><th>Self-sabotage</th><th>Root belief (Extra examples)</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>A loving, committed relationship</td><td>Not putting myself out there; running away when it gets difficult</td><td>I'll get hurt · they'll leave me · I'm not worthy of love</td></tr><tr><td>Starting my own business</td><td>Distracting myself, procrastinating, getting overwhelmed, focusing on what doesn't matter</td><td>If I fail, it proves I'm not good enough; everyone will find out I'm a fraud (imposter syndrome)</td></tr><tr><td>Losing weight</td><td>Overeating, binge eating then beating myself up</td><td>I'm not attractive, worthy or good enough · last time I felt attractive I got unwanted attention or got hurt, so this keeps me safe</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

### Go a level deeper

<p><b>It's not fear of failure; it's fear of what failure means to you.</b> If failure means learning and having a go, it's no problem. If it means "I've proved I'm unworthy, stupid, never going to be anything" (perhaps something an uncle once said), you'll avoid it. Likewise, "it's not success that's the problem, it's what it means to you."</p>

<p>In the worked example the practitioner drills down with: <b>"What's the problem with that?"</b> → <b>"And if that happened, what would it validate about yourself that you believe?"</b></p>

### The seven-year-old

<p>"Hands up if you are seven right now." Nobody. "Hands up if you <i>were</i> seven." Everyone. One day you were seven, then you woke up eight: a cake, candles, presents. No tears, no ritual, no breakout group. <b>You simply stopped believing "I am seven".</b></p>

<ul><li><b>You can't break through a belief, but you can uninstall it.</b></li><li><b>The filing cabinet:</b> one set of drawers holds your <b>Current</b> beliefs; another holds your <b>Old</b> ones (Santa Claus, the Easter Bunny). Belief change moves "I'm not good enough" into the old drawer.</li><li>"Sadly, most people believe more in Santa Claus than they do in themselves."</li><li>Belief work removes the belief; <b>Emotional release technique</b> and <b>Parts integration</b> (taught later) deal with the remaining feelings and behaviours.</li></ul>

## Part B: What beliefs are

<ul><li>A belief <b>Sets in stone</b> your thoughts, feelings and ideas about an area of life. Life is fluid and full of contradictions, so <b>Anything you attach to becomes limiting</b>.</li><li><b>Every belief can limit.</b> A $60,000 income target is empowering compared with $50,000, but limiting compared with $70,000, $100,000 or a million.</li><li>So think <b>Empowering vs. disempowering</b>, not good vs. bad (as in Chapter 1, Principle 4).</li><li>Common areas: <b>Money, relationships, success, work, balance, age, gender</b> (and food, exercise, sex, religion: the topics "you don't discuss at the dinner table").</li><li>"That's not a belief, that's just reality." "No, that's your belief about reality." Keep that <b>Detachment</b>.</li></ul>

## The model: A → B, then C → D

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th></th><th>Part 1: REMOVE</th><th>Part 2: INSTALL</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Elicit</td><td><b>A</b> = the current limiting belief ("I'm not good enough")</td><td><b>C</b> = a universal, absolutely true belief ("The sun will rise tomorrow" or "It's good to breathe")</td></tr><tr><td>Elicit</td><td><b>B</b> = an old belief that's no longer true: "<b>I am</b> a seven-year-old child"</td><td><b>D</b> = the new belief you want, ideally the <b>Opposite</b> of A</td></tr><tr><td>Map</td><td>Give A the submodalities of B</td><td>Give D the submodalities of C</td></tr><tr><td>Result</td><td>A now feels like a belief you <b>Used to</b> have</td><td>D now feels as certain as the sun rising</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<p><b>It's the like to dislike process done twice</b>, with beliefs instead of foods.</p>

## The script

<div class='script'><div class='script-row'><b>PART 1</b></div><div class='script-row'><b>Commitment and ecology:</b> "0 to 10, how committed are you to releasing it?" · "Is it okay with your unconscious mind for you to release this belief and have conscious awareness of it?" (wait for a congruent yes)</div><div class='script-row'>"Can you think of a limiting belief that you wish you didn't have? Good, what is it?" ... "As you think about that belief now, do you have a picture?" → <b>Elicit submodalities</b> (the whole checklist, top to bottom)</div><div class='script-row'><b>Clear the screen.</b></div><div class='script-row'>"Can you think of a belief that is no longer true? For example, you once had the belief 'I am a seven-year-old child', did you not? And do you believe that now?" ... "So when you think of that belief, where is that belief now? Do you have a picture?" → <b>Elicit submodalities</b></div><div class='script-row'>"Take that picture of that old belief and move it on the screen of your mind... put it right on top of that belief that is no longer true... and notice that it becomes..." (repeat each of B's submodalities, in <b>Command tonality</b>)</div><div class='script-row'>"You know the sound Tupperware makes? Just go ahead and lock it into place... <b>Thwack</b>." <b>Clear the screen.</b></div><div class='script-row'><b>Test:</b> "Now what do you think of that old belief?"</div><div class='script-row'><b>PART 2</b></div><div class='script-row'>"Can you think of a belief which for you is absolutely true? Like the belief that the sun is going to come up tomorrow, or that it's good to breathe. Good, what is it? As you think of that belief and how you know it's true, do you have a picture?" → <b>Elicit submodalities</b></div><div class='script-row'><b>Clear the screen.</b></div><div class='script-row'>"Can you think of a belief you want to have, which is the opposite of the old belief? Good, what is it? And as you think about that belief now, do you have a picture?"</div><div class='script-row'>"Take that picture of the new belief and put it right on top of the belief that is absolutely true... and notice that it becomes..." (repeat C's submodalities, with intensity; build it up)</div><div class='script-row'>"Lock it into place... <b>Thwack</b>." <b>Clear the screen.</b></div><div class='script-row'><b>Test:</b> "Now what do you believe? How do you know?" → future pace</div></div>

## Reading the test after Part 1: three responses

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Response</th><th>What it means</th><th>What to do</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>1. "I feel fantastic, so light, like a weight's lifted."</b></td><td>It worked</td><td>Celebrate, then go to Part 2</td></tr><tr><td><b>2. "Hmm... I don't know... I'm not sure."</b></td><td><b>Also a huge victory.</b> Their biggest belief has gone and they can't find it, like <b>Losing your car keys</b>. Or like a car in reverse: you have to stop and change gear before you can drive forward.</td><td>Reinforce it as a win. <b>Pre-frame this beforehand</b> so the client expects it.</td></tr><tr><td><b>3. "No, it's still there. I still believe it."</b></td><td>Rare: something in the process was missed</td><td>Run the process again. Even an imperfect run might take it from 10/10 to 3/10.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<div class='box key'><div class='box-title'>Key Takeaway</div><p><b>A belief is not an emotion.</b> If they say "I'm not sure how I feel", ask the logical question: <b>"Do you still believe that you're not good enough? Yes or no?"</b> No = "winner winner chicken dinner". Yes = repeat.</p>

</div>

## Tips for success

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Tip</th><th>Why</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>Two separate processes</b>: remove first, then install</td><td>If something goes wrong, you'll know which part it came from</td></tr><tr><td><b>Pre-frame the neutral response</b></td><td>Feeling lost or "put through a tumble dryer" is a win, not a failure</td></tr><tr><td><b>Speed</b></td><td>Go through the checklist quickly to bypass the conscious mind</td></tr><tr><td><b>Drivers: location and associated/dissociated</b></td><td>Have the client <b>Point with their hand</b> to where the image is. Change one driver and it will work; change both for a tremendous shift.</td></tr><tr><td><b>"I am" a seven-year-old, not "I was"</b></td><td>You still believe you <i>were</i> seven. The old belief is that you <i>are</i> seven right now.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Stay objective</b></td><td>Test the belief logically (yes or no), not the emotion</td></tr><tr><td><b>Part 2 is "half a job"</b></td><td>The new belief is "just a thought" with no full blueprint yet, so you mainly elicit the universal and apply it</td></tr><tr><td><b>Check for secondary gain</b></td><td>Sometimes people hold on to a belief because it keeps them safe. If the unconscious doesn't give a yes, don't push on.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Use your notes</b></td><td>Rip out a submodality checklist so you can write on it; read the script</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<p>The standard version shows the script as two parts; the practitioner describes it as "four parts" (A, B, C, D). Same thing.</p>

<p>Treat belief change as a coaching tool. Beliefs like "I'm worthless" can also be part of depression; if a client shows signs of that, suggest they also see a health professional.</p>

<div class='box extra'><div class='box-title'>Going deeper</div><p>Extra drill-down ("And if that happened, what would it say about you?") is very close to the <b>Downward arrow technique</b> used in cognitive therapy to find a <b>Core belief</b>.</p>

<p>Aaron Beck grouped core beliefs into three themes: <b>Helpless</b> ("I'm not good enough", "I'll fail"), <b>Unlovable</b> ("they'll leave me") and <b>Worthless</b>. Most of NLP training examples fit one of these.</p>

<p>In CBT, beliefs are changed by testing them against evidence (<b>Cognitive restructuring</b>) and through behavioural experiments. NLP belief change works on how the belief is <b>Coded</b> rather than its content; there's little formal research on it, so pairing it with real-world action is wise.</p>

<p>"The map is not the territory" (Korzybski, Chapter 3) is the idea behind "that's your belief about reality".</p>

<p>Carol Dweck's <b>Growth mindset</b> research shows beliefs about whether abilities can change affect effort and persistence, a well-studied example of how a belief shapes results.</p>
