# 31. Parts Integration

<p class='subtitle'>Resolving internal conflict: the method, the script and worked examples</p>

## Internal conflict

<p>Internal conflict happens when <b>Two parts of you compete</b>; you're "trying to serve two masters". One is the <b>Aspirational</b> part that wants goals and achievement; the other is pessimistic or fearful: "What if we try and fail and mess it all up?"</p>

<ul><li>"Part of me wants to be successful, but part of me worries success will come with a negative consequence."</li><li>"Part of me would love a beautiful relationship, but part of me is afraid of being hurt."</li><li>It's like <b>Driving fast with the handbrake on</b>.</li><li>The <b>Angel and devil</b> on your shoulders: one whispers "start your business, make an impact"; the other says "you'll fail, just like they told you". Listen to one and you move forward; listen to the other and you hold back.</li></ul>

## Write these down

<blockquote class='quote'>"Most behaviours are presenting problems."</blockquote>

<blockquote class='quote'>"A (self-sabotaging) behaviour is an outward manifestation of an internal conflict."</blockquote>

<ul><li>Most people try to fix the <b>Behaviour</b>. "Why do we keep fighting?" The fighting wasn't the problem; the unconscious issues were.</li><li><b>Weight</b> reflects habits, self-worth and self-discipline. Example: feeling "not good enough" → overworking → no time to eat well or exercise → weight gain. Lose weight without resolving the internal issue and it comes back.</li><li><b>The orange tree:</b> you want apples, so you pick off all the oranges and hope apples grow. They won't. Even if you chop the tree down, the <b>Roots</b> remain and it grows back.</li><li>So: <b>Dissolve the conflict</b> so you can move forward with alignment and purpose. (This is the iceberg from Chapter 2: presenting problem vs. root cause.)</li></ul>

## How parts integration works

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Stage</th><th>What happens</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>1. ### The principle underneath: every behaviour has a positive intention

<p>"Everything happens for a positive reason" isn't just a bumper sticker. Unconsciously, everything we do serves <b>Survival</b> and living our best life. Sometimes we do negative things to <b>Avoid pain</b>:</p>

<blockquote class='quote'>You self-sabotage → so you don't fail → so you're safe → so you can live your best life and feel fulfilled and free.</blockquote>

<p>It feels obvious when you read it, but in the exercise it feels completely new, because the conflict was <b>Subconscious</b>. "It's not an intelligent process, it's an emotional process."</p>

### Why the hands move

<ul><li>After a while with eyes closed, the client <b>Loses track of where their hands are</b>, like waking up with a "dead" leg and not knowing where it is in the sheets. They don't notice their hands moving.</li><li>Like a <b>Chiropractor</b> bringing the body into alignment so it can heal, bringing the parts into alignment lets the hands (and the person) come together.</li><li>Some clients integrate <b>In under 5 minutes</b>; some take <b>Up to 20</b>.</li></ul>

<div class='box key'><div class='box-title'>Key Takeaway</div><p><b>"Resistance is futile."</b> Keep showing up and keep reading the script (it's long, but "if you literally just read the words on the page, the client will transform"). If you're more persistent than the resistance, the client will integrate.</p>

<p>Most behaviours are presenting problems; self-sabotage is an <b>Outward sign of an inner conflict</b>. Find each part's positive intention, chunk up until they agree, and let them integrate.</p>

## Parts conflict and the full script

<p>This is called one of the most common exercises he does, with some of the biggest impact. A parts conflict <b>Looks like indecision</b>, but it isn't "chicken or fish?". It's a <b>Values conflict</b> between wanting something and a part that doesn't believe you <b>Deserve it, are worthy of it, or can make it happen</b>.</p>

<ul><li><b>Family echo:</b> if Mum and Dad disagreed (e.g. one parent heartfelt and loving, the other stern and wealth-focused: work harder or spend time with family? soft love or tough love?), that split can live on inside you.</li><li>If you're self-sabotaging or not taking action, "nine times out of ten" there's a parts conflict.</li><li><b>Listen for:</b> "I don't believe...", "I want to, but I can't", "It's not possible." These are perceptions and judgements, not facts.</li><li><b>Personification:</b> each part becomes a little figure on a hand, like toy soldiers or two <i>Toy Story</i> characters. It's not that person, just a representation of them. You're the referee, running "family therapy" inside the client.</li></ul>

### Finding the conflict: goal + limiting behaviour

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Part of me wants to...</th><th>but part of me keeps...</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Get in shape and lose weight</td><td>Overeating</td></tr><tr><td>Be in a relationship</td><td>Holding back, not putting myself out there</td></tr><tr><td>Become wealthy and financially free</td><td>Spending on silly things, or not investing out of fear of failing</td></tr><tr><td>Succeed in my career</td><td>Not speaking up in team meetings (afraid of rejection)</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

### Chunking up the problem part (Extra example)

<blockquote class='quote'>Not speaking up at work → so you're not judged → safe → protected → secure → enjoy life → free → fully express yourself → experience love and the fullest expression of life</blockquote>

<p>The career part chunks up to the <b>Same place</b>: experiencing everything life has to offer, feeling loved. Same intention, different strategy.</p>

### The script steps

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>#</th><th>Step</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1</td><td>Identify the conflict and the parts involved (goal + limiting behaviour). Write them down for the client.</td></tr><tr><td>2</td><td>Bring the part representing the <b>Unwanted</b> state or behaviour out onto one hand (left or right, it doesn't matter).</td></tr><tr><td>3</td><td>Get the <b>VAK</b> of it: "Who does this part most remind you of? Yourself (past, present or future), a friend, or a family member?" Give it a nickname.</td></tr><tr><td>4</td><td>Bring the <b>Opposite</b> part (the flip side of the coin) out onto the other hand; get its VAK and nickname.</td></tr><tr><td>5</td><td>Elicit the <b>Highest positive intention</b> of the unwanted behaviour. <b>Separate the intention from the behaviour</b> (we judge others by their behaviour, ourselves by our intentions). Keep asking "For what purpose?", looping higher each time.</td></tr><tr><td>6</td><td>Do the same for the other part. As soon as the hands move: <b>"You're not doing that on purpose, are you?"</b></td></tr><tr><td>7</td><td>Point out that <b>Both have the same highest positive intention</b>; get the client to acknowledge it. Point out the <b>Resources</b> each could offer the other.</td></tr><tr><td>8</td><td>Have the parts notice they were once <b>Part of a larger whole</b>, and want to work together.</td></tr><tr><td>9</td><td>Ask: <b>"Is there anything else that would like to join this integration?"</b> About 7 in 10 times the answer is no. Sometimes another part joins: a family member, a friend, or a quality like <b>Forgiveness</b> or <b>Non-judgement</b>.</td></tr><tr><td>10</td><td>Allow the hands to come together and integrate deep inside; bring them over the heart, take deep breaths, anchor it.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<p><i>This is called a 12-step script. These are all the steps describes.</i></p>

### Facilitation tips

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Tip</th><th>Why</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>Arms extended</b>, not tucked in (not rigid). If they sag, gently lift them.</td><td>A natural tendency for the hands to move together</td></tr><tr><td><b>Chunk up high enough</b>: get through at least <b>Two "aha" breakthroughs</b></td><td>Surface answers won't reveal the shared intention</td></tr><tr><td>If the client loops: <b>"All of that, for what purpose?"</b></td><td>Moves them up a level</td></tr><tr><td><b>Don't touch the hands until they've touched</b></td><td>With eyes closed they lose spatial awareness; the moment of touch is a powerful integration</td></tr><tr><td><b>Be patient</b></td><td>These parts may have been in conflict for 20, 30 or 40 years; you're integrating them in about 15 minutes. If they're committed and you read the script, they will integrate.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

### Boundary conditions

<p>When chunking up to the highest positive intention, go through at least <b>Two boundary conditions</b>: moments when the client pauses, loops back, or says "umm" and "ahh" about the answer. A boundary condition means they're at the edge of what they've consciously thought about, which is exactly where the true highest intention is found. If they're stuck: <i>"You're right where you need to be. You're at a boundary condition, which means you're about to have a breakthrough."</i></p>

### The full script, word for word

<p>Set-up sentence: <i>"Part of me wants to achieve <b>(goal)</b>, but part of me self-sabotages by <b>(problem behaviour)</b>, because deep down I <b>(root cause)</b>."</i> The root cause is often not feeling good enough or worthy, or not believing you can have what you want and keep something you already have ("Part of me wants to run a business, part of me wants to be a good parent, and I don't feel like I can do both").</p>

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>#</th><th>Stage</th><th>Say / do</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1</td><td><b>Commitment</b></td><td>"What's the parts conflict?"</td></tr><tr><td>2</td><td></td><td>"How is that a problem for you now?"</td></tr><tr><td>3</td><td></td><td>"What is it preventing you from doing that you really want to do?" Challenge them. <b>10:</b> continue.</td></tr><tr><td>4</td><td><b>Part 1: the problem part</b></td><td>"So part of you wants ___ (goal), and another part of you wants / doesn't believe ___ (behaviour / root cause)."</td></tr><tr><td>5</td><td></td><td>"I wonder if I could talk to the part of you that's ___ (problem behaviour), and I wonder if that part would like to come out and stand on one of your hands. ## Worked example: two parts in conflict

<p>Goal: <b>Self-love</b> and a <b>Healthy, happy relationship</b>. Blocking part: <b>Doesn't believe she's good enough, negative self-talk, puts up walls</b>.</p>

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th></th><th>Right hand: wants self-love and a happy relationship</th><th>Left hand: the walls and negative self-talk</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Position / weight</td><td>Middle; <b>Heavy</b></td><td>Back; <b>Light</b> ("wasn't what you expected")</td></tr><tr><td>Reminds her of</td><td>Her younger sister, <b>The client</b> ("younger and wiser")</td><td><b>The client</b> (herself)</td></tr><tr><td>What it says</td><td>"You deserve to be loved."</td><td>"Don't get out of your comfort zone. <ul><li>When she said "I don't know", the practitioner redirected: <b>"We don't ask yourself, we ask the client."</b></li><li>She asked whether the negative self-talk would stop. The practitioner: it was a way of <b>Protecting</b> her; with a teammate, the client can relax a little.</li><li><b>Bridging the gap:</b> "Who would reach out the hand of friendship?" "the client, since she's the one that put the walls up." Then the client embraces her.</li><li>No other parts wanted to join. Hands to the heart: "Don't rush the process." Her reaction: "It's like I'm having a hot flush... like a hot shower."</li></ul>

## Worked example

<p><b>Before you start</b>: be clear on the <b>Goal</b> and <b>How you self-sabotage</b>. The root cause matters less; "we'll figure out the rest together". People mess this process up by "dabbling around at the beginning". Then follow the script on <b>Pages 68–71</b>: goal and problem → nicknames → highest positive intentions → learn and share.</p>

<p><b>The client's situation:</b> two months into starting a coaching business (target: about nine months), because he wants to give the money away and <b>Contribute</b>. Doubt is creeping in: fear of failure, fear of not being good enough. Commitment 10/10; ecology question: "Yeah, totally."</p>

<p><b>Finding the behaviour</b> ("If I had a GoPro on you, what would I see you doing?"): anxiety, tightness, distraction, getting lost, thrown off direction. The practitioner bundles these into a list of <b>Trigger words</b> to use throughout.</p>

### What the practitioner did, step by step

<ul><li>Camera set so he could see the client's hands and face; arms out, palms flat.</li><li>Placed each part, then asked: front, middle or back of the hand? Heavy or light? Who does it remind you of? What would it say to you?</li><li>Started with the <b>Problem part</b> (Mum). When the client got stuck, the practitioner used <b>"I know you don't know, but if you did know..."</b> (Chapter 1).</li><li>Then chunked up the client. Both arrived at <b>Fulfilment</b>. "Do they realise they both want the same thing, and have been in conflict?"</li><li>"Would they be willing to work together <b>Inside you</b> so you can feel fulfilled?" ("You don't have to be the United Nations": whether they talk in real life is up to them.)</li><li>The hands started moving. The practitioner checked: <b>"You're not doing that on purpose, are you?"</b> "No, I didn't notice."</li><li>Wisdom and emotional gifts exchanged both ways. Hands shaking and coming closer: "the more deeply you breathe..."</li><li>Before integrating: <b>"Is there any other part of you that would like to come into this integration and heal itself now?"</b> the client: <b>Hurt</b>. Hurt came to stand with the client, who put an arm around it. The client: "It's the client saying it's okay to let it out."</li><li><b>Colour of fulfilment:</b> dark, blood red. A silk ribbon of that colour wraps the hands, "tighter and tighter with every breath".</li><li>Hands together → <b>Over the heart</b> → the red fills him "from the tips of your toes to the longest hair on your head", integrating "in the place reserved for all such learnings". They loved you and want to support you.</li></ul>

<div class='box key'><div class='box-title'>Key Takeaway</div><p>The two parts were the client's <b>Granddad</b> and his <b>Mum</b>, but the practitioner reminds him: "It's not your mum, it's your internal alignment." The parts are inside you.</p>

<p>Extra moves to remember: check they aren't moving their hands on purpose; invite <b>Any other part</b> to join before integrating; use the <b>Colour of the shared intention</b> as a ribbon.</p>

<div class='box extra'><div class='box-title'>Going deeper</div><p>In classic NLP this is the <b>Visual squash</b> (Bandler and Grinder, late 1970s), and "every behaviour has a positive intention" is one of NLP's core presuppositions.</p>

<p><b>Internal Family Systems</b> (IFS), a therapy developed by Richard Schwartz, also treats the mind as made of "parts" that each have a protective, positive intention, and helps them work together rather than fight.</p>

<p>In <b>Motivational interviewing</b> (Miller and Rollnick), this "part of me wants to, part of me doesn't" feeling is called <b>Ambivalence</b>, and resolving it is treated as the key to change. Exploring both sides with curiosity, as parts integration does, is central to that approach.</p>

<p>The hands moving "by themselves" is known as the <b>Ideomotor effect</b>: small, unconscious muscle movements that follow what we're thinking about (the same effect behind Ouija boards and pendulums). It's a real effect, which is why the coach must never pull the hands together.</p>
