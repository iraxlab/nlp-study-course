# 32. Emotional Release Technique (ERT)

<p class='subtitle'>Releasing a negative emotion at its first occurrence: explanation, method and a worked example</p>

<p>The practitioner calls ERT "by far one of the most powerful techniques" in the toolkit. With a prepared, committed client it takes about <b>3–5 minutes</b>. It's for the <b>Big, heavy</b> negative emotions.</p>

<blockquote class='quote'>"It's the emotional <b>Release</b> technique, not the 'emotional let me relive my trauma' technique."</blockquote>

## Where it comes from

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Source</th><th>Idea</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>Gestalt therapy</b> (Fritz Perls)</td><td>Experiences of the same emotion <b>Stack</b> over time into a pattern (a "gestalt"), starting from a first occurrence.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Time Line Therapy</b> (Tad James)</td><td>A later version: travel back along your timeline to the first event, take the learnings, and the emotion is released in the present.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Hindsight</b></td><td>We all have experiences that hurt at the time but that we're now grateful for. ERT does on purpose what time does eventually: "Time is a great healer. NLP is also a great healer, it's just more efficient."</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

### Three metaphors

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Metaphor</th><th>Meaning</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>String of pearls</b></td><td>Each experience of the emotion is a pearl on a string. Cut off the <b>First (bottom) pearl</b> and they all fall off.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Contact tracing</b></td><td>Find "patient zero" (the first experience) and deal with it there, so the "virus" doesn't spread.</td></tr><tr><td><b>An onion</b></td><td>Work from the outside in: release the primary emotions in order.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<blockquote class='quote'>"Management does not equal mastery."</blockquote>

<p>There's a difference between <b>Experiencing anger and being an angry person</b>, between <b>Being depressed and having depression</b>, and between <b>Feeling anxious and having an anxiety disorder</b>.</p>

## The five primary negative emotions (in order)

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>1</th><th>2</th><th>3</th><th>4</th><th>5</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>ANGER</b></td><td><b>SADNESS</b></td><td><b>FEAR</b></td><td><b>HURT</b></td><td><b>GUILT</b></td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<ul><li>Like the <b>Primary colours</b>: the foundations of all other negative emotions.</li><li><b>Always start with anger.</b> To release fear: anger → sadness → fear. To release guilt: all five in order. ("Reverse stack" them.)</li><li>Choosing an emotion <b>Not</b> on the list? Go ahead (but if anger is also an issue, do anger).</li><li>When you write your emotion down, write e.g. "<b>Fear AKA anger</b>" so you know to start with anger.</li><li><b>You can't release "trauma"</b>: it's a generalisation, like doing a like to dislike on "sugar". Pick the <b>Specific emotion</b> the trauma brings up (hurt, sadness, anxiety, guilt...).</li></ul>

## Safety rules (very important)

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>Rule</th><th>Detail</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>Ecology first, and it must be yes</b></td><td>"Is it okay with your unconscious mind for you to release this emotion and have conscious awareness of it?" If <b>No, don't do the process</b>: you don't know what's attached to the emotion.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Commitment</b></td><td>"0–10, how committed are you? Why?" Ask what the emotion has been costing them.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Stay dissociated</b></td><td>The client floats up "into the heavens and even higher than that", looking down so events are <b>Small and insignificant</b>. They <b>Never go into</b> the experience.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Place of safety</b></td><td>Go to <b>One hour before</b> the first event "or any of the events that led to that first event".</td></tr><tr><td><b>If they get emotional</b></td><td>Shift their state immediately: take them higher ("up to the moon") so they're above it. Tell them beforehand they can open their eyes and stop at any time.</td></tr><tr><td><b>Aftercare</b></td><td>Drink plenty of water, sleep well, less screen time, meditate, journal, read. Old memories may bubble up: "bless them and release them". Check in the next day. <b>Don't do too many emotions in one go.</b></td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## The method (as the practitioner does it)

<div class='table-wrap'><table><thead><tr><th>#</th><th>Step</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1</td><td>Identify the emotion; check commitment and ecology.</td></tr><tr><td>2</td><td><b>Associate</b> to it briefly: remember the last time you felt it (enough to identify it, not to be overwhelmed). Optional: give it a colour.</td></tr><tr><td>3</td><td><b>Float up</b> out of your body into the heavens, "and even higher than that". See your timeline: the future stretching one way, the past the other (the practitioner pictures a <b>Silver thread</b>).</td></tr><tr><td>4</td><td><b>Float back</b> to the very first time this emotion was an issue, "which when you disconnect it now, will cause it never to be an issue again". It doesn't matter if they consciously remember: trust the unconscious.</td></tr><tr><td>5</td><td>Ask: <b>"Was it before, during or after your birth?"</b> Usually it's after birth and <b>Before age 7</b>, most often <b>2–5</b>. Then go <b>A little further back</b> to the place of safety.</td></tr><tr><td>6</td><td><b>Learnings:</b> "What are the positive, empowering learnings you can take from this event, so you release the emotion and keep only the learnings?" Validate them; turn any negatives into positives.</td></tr><tr><td>7</td><td>Give the learnings a <b>Colour</b> and a <b>Sound</b>. Let them fill the body; store them "in the place you reserve for all such learnings".</td></tr><tr><td>8</td><td><b>The bowl of paint</b> (Extra addition): a big silver singing bowl filled with paint of that colour and sound. Dip your hands in, cover yourself, and on "3, 2, 1, <b>SWISH</b>" splash it through your whole timeline, past → present → future (two or three times).</td></tr><tr><td>9</td><td><b>Check:</b> "Look down at that first experience. What's happened to the old emotion? Is it still there or has it disappeared?" If it's still there, you haven't found the first time: go back again.</td></tr><tr><td>10</td><td>Float down with the leftover paint and splash it around the first event as an adult; float back up; return to the present bringing the learnings.</td></tr><tr><td>11</td><td><b>Future pace:</b> "Jump into the future, to a time when that old emotion would have shown up. How are you showing up now? How do you know?" Repeat if needed, then <b>Celebrate</b>.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<p>The practitioner mentions that if the first time is <b>Before birth</b>, some practitioners treat this as "past life regression"; he doesn't teach it here.</p>

## The script

<div class='script'><div class='script-row'><b>PART 1</b></div><div class='script-row'>"What's the emotion you would like to release now?" · "Is it okay with your unconscious mind for you to release this emotion now and have conscious awareness of it?" (<b>Must have alignment</b>) · "0 to 10, how committed are you to releasing this emotion now? Why?"</div><div class='script-row'>"Close your eyes and remember the last time you felt ___. Allow your unconscious mind to identify this emotion now, so we can easily find it."</div><div class='script-row'>"Now rise above the chair and soar into the heavens and even higher than that, so you're looking down on this experience. See the past stretched out in one direction and the future in the other."</div><div class='script-row'>"Allow your unconscious mind to guide you, easily and effortlessly, all the way back to the very first time this emotion was ever an issue for you, which when you disconnect from it now, will cause it to release and never be an issue for you again. It doesn't matter if you have conscious awareness of that first time; trust your unconscious now."</div><div class='script-row'>"Allow your unconscious to identify that exact time and take you to a place of safety, one hour before the event or any of the events that led to that event." (If the client becomes emotional, shift their state immediately.) "Are you in that place of safety now? Fantastic, you're doing so well."</div><div class='script-row'><b>PART 2</b></div><div class='script-row'>"Look down on that experience and ask yourself: what are the positive and empowering learnings I can take from this event, such that I will release the emotion and be left only with the positive learnings now?" (Validate. If negative, shift to the positive.) "Those are the perfect learnings."</div><div class='script-row'>"If those learnings had a colour, what colour would they be? That's the perfect colour. And if they had a sound? That's the perfect sound."</div><div class='script-row'>"See them fill your entire body and soul, from the tips of your fingers and toes to the longest hair on your head. Store them in the place you reserve for all such learnings, so you can draw from them whenever you need them."</div><div class='script-row'><b>PART 3</b></div><div class='script-row'>"In front of you, see a bowl of paint that's [colour], makes [sound], and is full of the learnings [__] and [__]. Dip your hands into the paint, and on the count of three, splash it through your entire stream of life, from the past into the present and into the future. Ready: 3, 2, 1, <b>SWISH</b>. Again: 3, 2, 1, <b>SWISH</b>."</div><div class='script-row'>"Now when you look down at that first experience, what happened to the old negative emotion? Is it still there, or has it disappeared now?" (If not gone, repeat from the start to find the first time.) "That's right, it has disappeared. How do you feel instead?"</div><div class='script-row'><b>PART 4</b></div><div class='script-row'>"How do you feel? Close your eyes and jump out into the future, to a time when in the past that old emotion would have shown up. How are you acting and engaging with the world differently? How do you know?" Repeat x2. <b>Celebrate!</b></div></div>

<div class='box key'><div class='box-title'>Key Takeaway</div><p><b>Release, don't relive.</b> Dissociated, up high, at a place of safety before the first event. Take the learnings; the emotion goes.</p>

<p>Primary emotions in order: <b>Anger, sadness, fear, hurt, guilt</b>. Always start with anger. Ecology must be a yes.</p>

</div>

## Worked example

"<b>It's fading... it's cleared.</b>" The feeling now: <b>Happy</b>.</td></tr><tr><td>Future pace</td><td>"Bright. It's clear." How do you know? "I can see it."</td></tr><tr><td>Fear check</td><td>"If fear was 10/10 at the start, where is it now?" "<b>It's gone.</b> I can't think of it actually." Releasing anger often releases the emotions stacked with it, so the practitioner stopped there.</td></tr><tr><td>Aftercare</td><td>Water, a good night's sleep, less screen time, meditate and journal; let old memories bubble up and release them; the practitioner will check in tomorrow and do sadness and fear if needed.</td></tr><tr><td>The client</td><td>"That one was massive... I hadn't spoken to anyone about it. I didn't think you'd be able to do that."</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<p>The practitioner: <b>"It's not the tool that's so powerful, it's your commitment to the tool that makes it powerful."</b></p>

<div class='box extra'><div class='box-title'>Going deeper</div><p><b>Imagery rescripting</b>, a well-researched therapy technique (Arntz and others), also revisits early memories with the client's adult perspective and changes their meaning. It has good evidence for several conditions, though it's done by trained therapists.</p>

<p>Finding positive learnings in hard experiences is studied as <b>Benefit finding</b> and <b>Post-traumatic growth</b> (Tedeschi and Calhoun).</p>

<p>The "look down from the heavens" step is a strong form of <b>Self-distancing</b> (Kross and Ayduk), which reduces emotional reactivity when recalling painful memories.</p>

<p>When an old memory is recalled it can be updated before it's stored again (<b>Memory reconsolidation</b>), one proposed reason why revisiting memories with a new perspective can change how they feel.</p>
